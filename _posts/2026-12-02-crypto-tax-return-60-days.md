There are 60 days left to file your Self Assessment tax return for the 2025–26 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026). If you had crypto in that year, here's what you need to know before 31 January 2027.

## Do you need to report your crypto?

You'll generally need to report crypto on your return if:

- your crypto gains were over the £3,000 tax-free allowance
- you had crypto income, such as staking, mining, airdrops or being paid in crypto
- you disposed of more than £50,000 of crypto in total, even if your gain was small

## It's not just selling

Swapping one coin for another is a disposal for Capital Gains Tax. So is spending crypto on goods or services, and gifting it, unless the gift is to your spouse or civil partner. For 2025–26, gains are taxed at 18% or 24%, depending on your income.

## Fill in the crypto boxes

The return has a separate crypto section in the capital gains pages. It asks for your number of disposals, your disposal proceeds and allowable costs, and your gains and losses for the year.

## Gather your records

- Statements from every exchange you used, including any that have closed
- Your wallet addresses, plus the dates, sterling values and fees for each transaction
- Calculations that follow HMRC's pooling and 30-day rules

## Late means penalties

- £100 straight away if your return is late, even if you owe nothing
- After 3 months, £10 a day, up to £900
- At 6 and 12 months, a further 5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is more
- If you pay late, there are 5% penalties at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest at the Bank of England base rate plus 4%

## Start this week

HMRC says around 5 million people leave their return until January. Filing early doesn't mean paying early: you still have until 31 January to pay. If you're employed and owe less than £3,000, filing online by 30 December 2026 lets you pay through your tax code.
